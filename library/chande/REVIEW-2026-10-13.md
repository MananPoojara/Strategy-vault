# Chande conversion candidate, 13 October 2026

This is a bounded review of two stop-free price contracts from **Beyond Technical Analysis** (1997). The working changes are staged for a different reviewer and deterministic publisher. They have not been committed, deployed or admitted into the live application. No entire-book completion or original performance reproduction is claimed.

The supplied complete BTA PDF was read using its text layer. The supplied **The New Technical Trader** PDF is a scan; spreads 89 and 100–102 were rendered and visually inspected. The page images, extracted text, original source hashes, observed inputs and generated reports remain in the private job directory outside Git. The unavailable OCR executable is recorded in that evidence. Public files contain interpreted rules and audit code only.

## Ready contracts

| Method ID | Source and defaults | Orders and exits | Family |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `ch_bta2_ma_stack` | BTA printed pp.20–21, PDF pp.29–30. Close SMA3 and SMA4 versus SMA65. | Buy at the signal close when **both** short SMAs are strictly above SMA65; short when both are strictly below. Opposing full agreement exits and reverses at that close. Mixed or equal agreement holds the current position. | `ch_ma_stack`; the printed SMA3-through-SMA7 option is a parameter variant. |
| `ch_bta2_65sma_ravi` | BTA printed p.80 and pp.94–97,99. SMA65, three consecutive closes, SMA7, RAVI barrier 1.0%. | RAVI is `abs(100*(SMA7-SMA65)/SMA65)`. After at least three closes strictly above/below their respective completed SMA65 and RAVI strictly above the barrier, enter or reverse at the next observed open. Weak RAVI alone does not exit. | Existing `ch_reversal` family; thresholds remain variants. |

For the SMA agreement contract, pp.20–21 describe two rules each for long and short, specify buying on the close, discuss delayed entries and exits, and explicitly exclude an initial stop. The interpretation uses symmetric full agreement as a reversal; it does not turn mixed agreement into an exit. Defaults `first=3,count=2,slow=65` are printed. `count=5` implements the printed 3-through-7 variant. Other allowed lookbacks are explicit research variants.

For RAVI, the contract combines the base next-open reversal execution on p.80 with the stop-free, always-invested filtered model illustrated in Figures 4.17 and 4.18, pp.96–97. The barrier 1.5% is printed in Figure 4.18. A 0.5% threshold is also checked as a **stop-free research variant**; it does not reproduce Table 4.4, which separately adds a $1,000 futures stop. The source's one-way restriction is inert without stop exits. Nonzero or nonfinite legacy `stop_pts` requests are rejected in both the runner and readiness check.

Both contracts use only complete observed SMA windows. Each test begins flat at its selected start, while keeping admitted earlier observations for indicators. Same-direction signals do not add positions. There is one unit and one sequential position per actual symbol, without portfolio allocation or pyramiding. A terminal position is marked at the last observed close with `open=true`; it is not presented as a realized exit. On-close orders can execute on the final bar. A final RAVI next-open order remains unfilled when no next observed bar is available.

No numeric default is missing in these two contracts. No ATR default was needed. Neither contract invents a fixed dollar stop, contract tick, multiplier, rollover, auxiliary series or daily intrabar path. Close-based fills are the source's idealized daily paper execution convention; actual broker execution is not asserted.

## Research application and coverage

The original examples discuss futures. The selected actual price series are declared cash/ETF/stock research applications using scale-independent moving averages and percentage conditions. NIFTY cash-index short exposure is hypothetical. Price returns do not reproduce futures contract P&L, dollar commissions, roll effects, borrowing, financing or dividend accounting. Input adjustment bases are retained as supplied.

The common requested comparison period is 1 January 2010 through 11 September 2020. The actual first tradeable observation in that interval is 4 January 2010 on each selected series.

## Independent ledger verification

The diagnostic Rust driver compiles the **actual** `chbta2.rs`, `chntt.rs` and `connors.rs` files. It emits full chronological trade records, including entry/exit indices, direction, prices, entry mode, weight, exit signal index, reason and terminal-open flag. It does not provide replacement strategy calculations.

The separately written Python oracle computes Decimal means from each complete source-price window and builds a source-order event table. It imports no Rust logic. It checks every chronological record, prices to an absolute tolerance of 1e-8, exact dates/indices, direction, fill mode, open flag, reason and weight. Separate events identify suppressed and unfilled signals. Generated Rust/oracle ledgers retain dates and distinguish a terminal mark from an exit fill.

The bounded suite has 24 real ledger comparisons: two contracts, three parameter variants each and four actual series. It also checks causal prefixes and truncates each real RAVI sample on its first actionable signal to demonstrate that the absent next open cannot fill. Synthetic fixtures cover mixed agreement, equality, thresholds, warmup, final-bar close reversal, sample-end orders and prestart orders; they are diagnostic only and excluded from real checked-trade totals.

Reproduction uses a runtime job directory containing the source review and the supplied four dataset specifications:

```bash

python3 "$CH_JOB/oracle-scripts/verify_prices.py" --root "$PWD" --job "$CH_JOB" --binary "$CH_JOB/actual-price-harness"
```

`CH_JOB` is a task-specific runtime path, not a source/data directory. Source and input files are read-only. The handoff records exact executable commands, source/input/module/binary hashes, row mismatch reports and test logs. The ledger report's flags and counts apply to these two contracts only. A zero-trade diagnostic is never the sole verification of a method.

## Held methods and exclusions

All other `chbta2`/`chntt` methods are excluded from this ready subset. The private handoff enumerates every held ID and its reason. In particular:

- BTA fixed-dollar-stop experiments, S&P absolute price thresholds and futures tick barriers require actual contracts, point/tick values and roll prices. Turning dollars into Nifty points, disabling a required stop or using a generic cash tick does not establish those source systems.
- Intermarket systems require their actual gold/bond/commodity inputs. Variable-contract pullback and Long Bomb systems require their source contract-dollar sizing. Missing data or sizing are not replaced by one-unit index tests.
- The ADX burst, DMI-filter, combined-exit and profit-triggered exit examples retain unresolved order/exit/measurement requirements. A configurable numeric value alone does not resolve those contracts.
- NTT CMO-driven VIDYA, printed pp.180–183 (PDF spreads 101–102), uses a one-contract-tick stop entry and a $1,500 initial stop. The suggestion of closing-based stock trading does not specify a cash substitute for that dollar stop. Its readiness is held for actual contract inputs and causal execution evidence.
- NTT's thrust table, printed p.157 (PDF spread 89), supplies a next-close buy but no executable exit. Its 3/6/12/24-month observations are event-study horizons. The historical month-hold runners retain their existing catalogue status and are blocked by the unchanged source-review instrument policy; neither a sequential account nor an NSE breadth replacement is invented.
- Other NTT indicator and rotation ideas remain held without complete orders, exits or allocation.

`ch_65sma3cc` and `ch_cbpb_time` are excluded and unchanged. All prior instrument admissions are preserved. Exact staged review additions name only the two ready contracts and their book ID; no other Chande method inherits compatibility. Frontend, authentication, gateway, configuration, workers, `main.rs`, `connors.rs` and original source/data shares are unchanged.

The mandated hook installation was attempted and failed because `.git/config` is read-only in this managed working copy. No Git metadata was changed. Review and publication remain separate steps.
