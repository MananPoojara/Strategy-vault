## Shared research execution contract

Each test starts flat at its selected start. Earlier actual bars warm indicators and, where needed, setup state; no pre-window account position is invented. Nifty history is restricted to dates from January 2000. One position is tracked per actual instrument, with no pooled stock population, leverage, funding, dividends, futures basis, contract rolls or portfolio allocation inferred. Close fills retain the CD convention where it is given. They are daily research fills, without a modeled auction or intraday slippage path.

The existing close-order builder requires a new entry before the selected final bar. A final-close entry is suppressed; an opposite signal at that close liquidates the old position without creating a zero-duration new position. A remaining position is marked at the final close with `open=true`, and is excluded from closed-trade statistics. The two-band system can fill a preceding close's market order at the final observed open, then mark its remainder. It never fills a final-close order with an open outside the selected window.

For the five adaptive functions, the CD driver's signal predicate is:

```text
change[t] = trend[t] - trend[t-1]
SD[t] = population standard deviation of change[t-20] ... change[t-1]
CurrentBar > period+5
abs(change[t]) > sdfilter*SD[t] AND abs(change[t]) > mfilter
positive eligible change -> long; negative eligible change -> short
fill/reverse at this close; no pyramiding
```

`CurrentBar` is interpreted as the one-based observed bar number. Both comparisons are strict. The prior 20-change window excludes today's change and must be complete and finite. The CD supplies `period=10`, `sdfilter=.1`, `mfilter=0`; these are the tested baseline defaults. Nonzero `mfilter` is an instrument-local point **research** threshold, not a printed USD amount converted to INR. Changing its threshold, direction, period or selected market does not add a method.

TradeStation MaxBarsBack settings and prehistory were not supplied. The initial-history conventions below therefore remain declared adaptations. The tests certify the implemented conventions and source predicates, not original-platform seed parity. No missing prices or history are fabricated.

The two-band ledger retains each partial fill's bar, price and fraction. The engine's `Trade.exits` convention includes a final terminal mark piece when a partially exited position stays open; `open=true` identifies that final piece as a **mark**, not an executed sale. Single full exits are stored directly in the normal exit fields. The engine has an entry-kind field and an exit reason rather than a separate exit-kind field; the audit preserves the raw reason and annotates the exit fill mode from the actual runner branch. This representation is documented rather than silently treating a mark as a fill.

## Implementation changes and verification

VIDYA previously substituted the close when its longer historical window was unavailable. This could send close-direction trades before the required volatility ratio existed. It now leaves those values unavailable, seeds once at the first full window, and handles zero historical variance by carrying the previous trend. Parameter validation requires historical period greater than recent period and First Cross fast average shorter than slow average.

The partial-exit system's `signal_i` now points to the preceding close for completed next-open exits and to the selected final bar for terminal marks. The independent reviewer corrected the outside-day runner to liquidate an existing position on an opposite qualifying final close while suppressing the new position beyond the selected window. The Adaptive RSI extension uses the existing verified Wilder RSI implementation. All shared ATR arithmetic and the previous17 methods' calculations remain unchanged. Catalogue family keys describe complete research contracts; family registration or admission counts do not establish new original book algorithms.

The independently written [verify_adaptive_batch.py](scripts/verify_adaptive_batch.py) expresses the source predicates in40-digit Decimal arithmetic and builds its own position ledger. It shares only input parsing, hashing and elementary arithmetic utilities with the previous audit. It compiles the **actual** `books/kftsm.rs` and `connors.rs`, rather than a second Rust implementation. Its report hashes those exact absolute module files, the compiled harness/binary, the oracle and utility, PDF/CD files and all actual OHLC inputs.

The audit covers **40 baseline cases:10 methods ×4 real series**, requested January2010 through September11,2020. Actual selected trading dates are January4,2010 through September11,2020 for every series. Warmup begins January3,2000 for Nifty50 and RELIANCE, January29,1993 for SPY, and December12,1980 for AAPL. RELIANCE/AAPL are actual single stocks; SPY is the actual ETF; Nifty50 is the declared Indian cash-index research application. A selected/current constituent is not a historical index universe, and vendor adjustment quality is not independently certified.

**All10,015 chronological records match**, with zero differences in record counts, entry/exit bar indices and dates, direction, prices within the declared tolerance, entry and annotated exit fill modes, signal indices, partial fill fractions, or terminal-open status. Every baseline has nonzero trades. Price tolerance is max(1e-8 absolute,1e-12 relative); the oracle's arithmetic is independent of the engine's floating calculations.

## Held methods and duplicate review

`kftsm_donchian_4wk` remains held. PDF177 specifies prior **full calendar** weeks and intraday stop/reversal. A daily bar can cross both bounds, including after a new position is entered or reversed. The source does not establish the old runner's skip-flat-both-hit/one-reversal-only convention. No close-only replacement is presented as the original rule, no intraday path is guessed, and no futures roll is inferred for cash prices. Its calendar-week ancestor also appears as `aanwr_weekly_rule`; `cov_tc_4week_reversal` uses20 trading days and is related rather than calendar-identical. These are not new algorithms counted here.

`kftsm_parabolic` is deferred. Its actual Ch17 SAR function and PDF274–275,609 were inspected, but no independent order-wrapper/fill ledger was completed in this batch. The CD function uses the prior bar's high/low in its continuation update; the prose describes the held-trade extreme. These must not be silently equated. The Wilder SAR family already appears as `rwmts_psar` and other catalogue methods; a fresh Kaufman registration would not add a new algorithm. Existing admission stays closed.

`kftsm_pf` remains held because the old5-point box comes from source e-mini S&P/NQ settings and does not establish local cash/stock execution units. Futures final-three-month, event, intraday, auxiliary-series, discretionary and other unreviewed methods retain their own holds. No blanket Kaufman or keyword admission is introduced.

The Ch05 and Ch08 copies of the same close-scaled swing function count **once**. The extremum-scaled, before-update swing is materially different, not a threshold variant. The filtered exponential driver reuses the Chapter8 smoothing primitive but adds a dynamic prior-change SD predicate, making a distinct complete source contract. KAMA's primitive is shared with `kfst_ama` (*Smarter Trading*); that system tracks movement away from prior trend extrema, while this driver tests one-day trend change, so their complete entry rules differ. No extra count is assigned for primitive reuse, parameter or direction variants, or additional market runs.
