# Bandy QTS source and independent implementation review — 14 October 2026

Howard B. Bandy, *Quantitative Trading Systems: Practical Methods for Design, Testing and Validation*, Blue Owl Press, 2007. The supplied scanned PDF has 367 physical pages. Title and copyright pages 4–5 establish the 2007 first printing: ISBN-13 9780979183805 and a printing number line ending in 1. Inspected physical strategy pages show the same printed page number. Original companion AFL and both adjacent errata were read independently of conversion conclusions.

This review accepts **13 existing catalogue IDs** as bounded individual-symbol research interpretations. It does not add 13 algorithms: the 5/25 and 1/36 moving-average cases share the existing moving-average family, and existing live corrections count as zero new methods. Parameter, direction, seed, instrument and driver extensions are declared research adaptations. The CGO chapter explicitly calls Figure 10.1 an entry/exit demonstration rather than a complete trading system; its mechanically specified companion exercise is reviewed without a claim to a new complete book algorithm. No source portfolio sizing, optimized walk-forward performance or book-reported statistics are reproduced. Publisher admission remains pending.

- The two MA cases buy a fast-over-slow Cross and sell the reverse Cross. The 1/36 values are active constants, not the commented optimizer defaults.
- Limit entry uses the previous bar’s 5/25 upward Cross, requires today’s Low strictly below 0.99 times yesterday’s Close, and expires after that bar. Sell is the reverse Cross at Close. A flat position can fill the earlier limit and sell at that bar’s Close. A carried long cannot refill an intraday limit before its close exit.
- Fixed interval buys when the absolute loaded BarIndex modulo 6 is zero; Sell is ExRem(BarsSince(raw Buy)>=3, Buy). The phase is not restarted at the selected date.
- Profit target keeps the literal scalar loop from bar zero. If flat it enters on a raw 5/25 Cross; otherwise High strictly above entry times 1.10 produces a target Sell. No target check occurs on the entry bar. There is no additional exit. Buy and Sell then use sequential ExRem.
- Entry identification uses a 3/16 Cross, BarsSince(raw Buy)>=6, then ExRem(Buy,Sell) and ExRem(Sell,Buy). Raw repeats while held restart the exit clock without adding exposure.
- Bollinger breakout uses MA(C,12) plus/minus 2.8 population standard deviations. Buy Cross(C,upper), Sell Cross(upper,C), Short Cross(lower,C), Cover Cross(C,lower). Both directions are in the companion code.
- CGO is zero initially; starting at bar 13 it weights the most recent 13 (High+Low)/2 prices by 1 through 13. The oscillator is 100 times (7 minus weighted sum / unweighted sum). It buys above DEMA(CGO,2), and sells on the reverse Cross or BarsSince(raw Buy)>=6, with ExRem on Sell only. The source’s SetBarsRequired(200,0) is exceeded by actual full-history warmup.
- Stochastic RSI uses RSIa(C,10), its 20-bar min/max range, DEMA of that normalized range with period 3, and opposite Cross signals. A zero range remains missing.
- DPO subtracts MA(C,30), smooths the result with DEMA(10), and crosses against DEMA of that smoother with period 3. It uses no displaced or future price.
- Calendar seasonality buys/covers on days 1–5, 10–16 and 21–31; sells/shorts on days 6–9 and 17–20. Held repeated day signals do not pyramid. Opposite signals reverse at the same Close.
- Daily MACD permission uses EMA(C,5)-EMA(C,37), smoothed with EMA(7). Permission requires its rise relative to the previous bar. Buy is Cross(MA(C,8),MA(C,7)) AND permission; Sell is the reverse Cross OR lost permission. The daily code contains no weekly compression.
- CMO sums up/down Close changes over 21 bars, normalizes their difference by their sum, and applies DEMA(96) and a DEMA(3) trigger. Exit is the reverse Cross or BarsSince(raw Buy)>=6; sequential ExRem follows. The source’s walk-forward example uses XLB ETF; this review applies the literal defaults to actual SPY ETF prices. It does not implement that optimizer or substitute stocks/index prices for ETFs.

## AFL timing, initialization and adaptations

Array order is oldest to newest. Ref(...,-1) reads the prior observed bar. Cross requires current strict superiority and prior less-than-or-equal, with finite operands. Equality and Null are not cross events. ExRem assignment order and raw Buy dependence of BarsSince are preserved. Arrays and the profit loop warm from the first real input row; the research account starts flat at the selected period.

Explicit zero SetTradeDelays and explicit BuyPrice/SellPrice remain literal. Figures that omit the settings use a declared zero-delay Close research selection consistent with pp.65–66 and 88. Short/Cover prices for calendar and Bollinger examples use Close. One position per symbol, no pyramiding, HoldMinBars=0, no artificial locked holding period. For the prior-day limit example AllowSameBarExit is enabled so a known intraday fill can exit on a later close signal. This does not invent an intraday path between unknown extrema.

PriceBoundChecking remains enabled: custom prices outside the bar range clamp to the nearest High/Low boundary. These are literal AFL backtest price-array fills, not a claim of exchange execution or opening-gap improvement. Target and limit touches use the source’s strict comparisons. No accepted figure uses ApplyStop. BarsSince exits are formula exits, not built-in N-bar stops. Built-in stops, activation, priority and volatile distance are not generalized to unreviewed figures.

The independent reviewer corrected two initialization differences in the exact approved IDs: MACD EMA now initializes from a full-period SMA; stochastic RSI follows AmiBroker’s published recurrence with P=N=0 from bar 1 and the first displayed ratio at period. DEMA initializes from its first finite input; population SD and full-window MA/HHV/LLV are used. Missing indicator output stays missing. Exact native AmiBroker export parity remains unproven; initialization/null conventions and a full-history independent Decimal comparison are recorded separately from that stronger claim. Primary initialization references: [EMA](https://www.amibroker.com/guide/afl/ema.html), [DEMA](https://www.amibroker.com/guide/afl/dema.html), and the published [RSI recurrence](https://www.amibroker.com/guide/afl/rsi.html). Array, timing and stop interpretations also use the official [Ref](https://www.amibroker.com/guide/afl/ref.html), [Cross](https://www.amibroker.com/guide/afl/cross.html), [ExRem](https://www.amibroker.com/guide/afl/exrem.html), [SetTradeDelays](https://www.amibroker.com/guide/afl/settradedelays.html), [ApplyStop](https://www.amibroker.com/guide/afl/applystop.html), and [SetOption](https://www.amibroker.com/guide/afl/setoption.html) references.

The independent reviewer also corrected the limit runner’s same-bar sequencing. Five actual bars have a valid limit fill followed by a Close Sell. They now close on that day; carried positions cannot exit and refill an earlier limit. The chosen research settings are explicit. This is a correction to an existing catalogue ID, not a new algorithm.
