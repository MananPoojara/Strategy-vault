# Selected high-volatility overnight stock application

This review covers only `kfsc_hv_day`, one declared reconstruction of the four-rule high-volatility subsystem in Perry Kaufman's *A Short Course in Technical Trading* (Wiley, 2003), printed pages 250–251. It is a selected stock research application. It does not reproduce the source's original AOL figure, stock population, reliability claim, capital programme or reported performance.

Primary PDF SHA256: `11dfd5d543d7bd0ab7c43a2e2f4a3593a7be9eee1c9a2fae124f6042601d857e`. Printed pages equal physical PDF pages minus 13. Source interpretation also uses printed 70, 83 and 243–255. No book or private market data is included here.

The exact selected rules are:

1. True range is the maximum of today's high minus low, high minus the actual previous stock close, and previous stock close minus low (printed 245). Row zero has no genuine previous close and no true range.
2. Use the arithmetic average of 65 complete true ranges ending today. Printed 244 includes today; printed 250 gives 65 days as an example. Choosing exactly 65 observations resolves imprecise N-days-ago wording. The earliest eligible source index is 65, requiring 66 genuine rows. Wilder smoothing and synthetic first-row closes are excluded.
3. Today's true range must be strictly greater than twice that inclusive average (printed 251). Equality does not signal.
4. A long signal requires `(close-low)/(high-low) >= .90`; a short signal requires `<= .10`. Selecting the upper decile explicitly corrects the inconsistent printed “upper 90 percent” wording using the clear-direction description and Figure 17.4. Inclusive zone boundaries are declared operational choices. Zero high-low range has an undefined fraction and no signal.
5. Do not enter on an actual Friday (printed 250). Weekday comes from the stock date, including holiday-shortened weeks.
6. Enter at the finalized signal-day close and exit at the following genuine market-session open. A prior overnight position exits at the open before any new signal enters at that session's close. These are idealized OHLC research fills: printed 70 recommends calculations before the close, printed 83's MOC/MOO orders allow any price within the last/first 60 seconds, and printed 250's prose says shortly after the open while Figure 17.4's caption selects the next open. Official daily close/open prices cannot certify those real fills.
7. A signal on the selected terminal session enters and remains open, even when the source file has future rows. It is not dropped or filled beyond the selected window. Any terminal liquidation cost remains an independently labelled estimate and is excluded from closed-trade count, sum and average.

The fixed exposed settings are 65 observations, factor 2, zone fraction .10 and current-window lag 0. Changed, unknown, malformed, nonfinite or unreviewed grid settings fail closed. There is no alternative-period optimization under this admission.

Actual stock observations must match their native market-session calendar over the required 65-TR warmup and selected period. Missing stock sessions cannot be treated as holidays or bridged to a later available open. Calendar defects outside that required interval do not imply missing selected stock data. Absent calendar coverage, invalid required OHLC/date chronology and unsupported decimal arithmetic hold the requested result.

Predicate comparisons use checked integer arithmetic on the shortest decimal representation of the loaded OHLC floats, up to 18 decimal places, without a price tick or epsilon. This roundtrip-decimal convention preserves supplied source decimals in the reviewed AAPL and RELIANCE inputs: all 66,700 OHLC values were checked against the original CSV text, with no changes (AAPL up to 8 places; RELIANCE up to 15). The 18-place bound applies to the loaded shortest-decimal values. The shared CSV loader has already parsed its original tokens to floats: other-stock original tokens with excess precision can have been rounded before this strategy sees them. Original-text identity is certified only for those checked AAPL/RELIANCE files; other stock inputs retain this explicit loader-precision qualification. No universal original-token fidelity is claimed. Scaling, summation or multiplication overflow holds the run. Approximate float TR/mean/fraction outputs accompany the exact `tr_above`, `upper_zone`, `lower_zone` and Friday predicates; readers should use those predicates instead of recomputing a boundary from displayed rounded values. These are calculated research states, not execution or book-performance certification. The typed entry order retains its source signal date. Generic `signal`/`at_exit` diagnostics associate a closed HV trade with its actual exit session; exit-open diagnostics omit that session's unavailable closing indicators. A terminal open holding has no filled `at_exit` snapshot.

Only actual individual stocks are admitted. An index, ETF or stock basket cannot inherit this review. Shorts remain hypothetical without certified locate, borrowing, margin or financing. Current membership snapshots, adjusted quotes and vendor price authenticity retain their existing qualifications.

Accounting retains the platform's normalized unit-allocation price-return model and linear per-side cost deductions. This is not a funded source-capital account. Gross and costed fills share the same quantities; closed-return sums and sequential compounded equity have distinct meanings. Existing terminal liquidation estimates remain separate from filled exits. General 2-TR profit targets, 3-TR stops and portfolio diversification/sizing examples on printed 248–255 are separate examples and have not been grafted onto the explicit four-rule subsystem.

Independent source chronology references were frozen before production math edits. Private receipts bind original inputs, source states, every trade, all daily outcomes, causal signal prefixes and zero/5-bps-per-side cases. Candidate validation and release integration remain distinct checks; a catalogue registration alone does not establish acceptance or deployment.
